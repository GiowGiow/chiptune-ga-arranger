A genetic algorithm that arranges songs for the NES sound chip

Every song has three renderings: the original input, the generated arrangement with General MIDI timbres, and the same arrangement on our NES chip synthesizer. The video plays the chip version and draws each channel as a glowing line.

For an ABC tune, the original is the lead sheet played plainly. For a MIDI song, it is the fan-made MIDI file itself, rendered with the same soundfont and cut to the same bars.

Reading the settings of each song

seed
Starts the random generator. The same seed and settings always give the same piece; another seed gives another arrangement of the same song.
fidelity weight
How much the fitness rewards keeping the melody in its written octave (default 0.5). At 0 the GA is free to move melody bars up or down an octave.
generations, stagnation
The GA stops after a maximum number of generations, or earlier when the best plan has not improved for a window of generations (default 3000 and 300; 4000 and 400 for the longer MIDI songs).
melody, bars, parts
For a MIDI song: the track read as the melody, the excerpt that is arranged, and the part labels; a chorus part gets a denser accompaniment through the contrast criterion.

Orange settings differ from the default configuration. The full list of hyperparameters is in the README.

The audio is rendered only from MIDI files: FluidSynth with the FluidR3_GM soundfont, or the project's own chip synthesizer (pulse, triangle and noise waveforms).
